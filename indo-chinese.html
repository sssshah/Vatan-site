The Tradition

What Is Indo-Chinese Food?

Indo-Chinese cuisine was born in Kolkata, home to India's oldest Chinese immigrant community, and has since become one of the most-ordered styles of food across India. It takes Chinese cooking techniques — high-heat wok tossing, cornstarch-thickened sauces, soy and vinegar bases — and layers them with Indian spices, green chilies, and garlic to create something entirely its own.

The result is bold, saucy, deeply savory food: crispy fried dumplings tossed in tangy Manchurian sauce, wok-tossed Hakka Noodles, and fiery Szechwan gravies. It's neither Chinese nor Indian in the traditional sense — it's a cuisine that belongs entirely to India.

At Vatan, our Indo-Chinese menu is 100% vegetarian — built around paneer, cauliflower, and fresh vegetables — with Jain and vegan options across most of the menu.

FAQ

Indo-Chinese Questions

What is Indo-Chinese food?

Indo-Chinese food is a fusion cuisine created by the Chinese immigrant community in Kolkata, India, blending Chinese cooking techniques with Indian spices. Dishes like Manchurian, Hakka Noodles, and Chilli Paneer are now staples on Indian restaurant menus nationwide.

Is Vatan's Indo-Chinese menu vegetarian?

Yes — every Indo-Chinese dish at Vatan is 100% vegetarian, made with paneer, cauliflower, and fresh vegetables instead of meat or eggs, in a fully vegetarian kitchen.

Do you have vegan Indo-Chinese options?

Yes. Several dishes, including Vegetable Manchurian, Chinese Bhel, Veg Hakka Noodles, and Szechwan Fried Rice, are vegan-friendly — look for the Vegan tag on our menu.

Is there a Jain-friendly Indo-Chinese option?

Yes — most of our Indo-Chinese starters and mains can be prepared Jain-style, without onion, garlic, or root vegetables. Look for the Jain tag or ask your server.

What's the difference between Manchurian and Hakka Noodles?

Manchurian refers to vegetables formed into fried dumplings and tossed in a tangy soy-based sauce, served dry or with gravy. Hakka Noodles are stir-fried wheat noodles tossed with vegetables in a lighter soy-ginger sauce.

Where can I get Indo-Chinese food near Jersey City or East Windsor NJ?
